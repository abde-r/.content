Task management tools simplify the organization of work within a professional environment, making the workflow of the different phases smooth and dynamic by reducing the complexity of demands and summing up with optimal strategies and optimized business plans. Also subtracting main components into sub-outlines and multi-frames. Depending on the variables of each objective, the nature of the team dealing with it, and their ability. Overall aiming to ease the process of from development to production.

<br />

Yet I personally find it completely miraculous the idea of how old Egyptians managed to release a giant, massive, and hyper-complicated project such as the Pyramids. No doubt if it was at this time, beside its grandeur and magnificence, it would definitively need a big infrastructure foundations practices and solid project management basis. However, tools that make these practices a piece of cake, and communication in a regular manner increasing productivity and enhancing outcome, also scheduling tasks to ease team work and enable parallel tasking. It was really done without even using any of them.

<br />

There must be certainly a huge hierarchy with a large number of people, in order to ensure the process of management and follow up the progress of the project, beside the numberless amount of workers and laborers. Grouped into units and each underlying under a speciality, exactly like departments in corporates. In order to simplify the connectivity between groups, each component headed by a supervisor, whom share the progress, advancements, and constraints of the crew.

<br />

In a project manager perspective, the bigger the team, the more complex the challenge became. Certainly releasing such a project back then with traditional tools and limited strategy resources was a genuine defy. The process of spawning blueprints and designing solutions is quite typical, but the actual difficulty is in fact during the execution phase. Like when it comes to sub-partitioning tasks by priority and availability, immediate plan B if things went down knowing the bigness of the structure, also ensuring the full engagement of each member of each group and monitor the progress. Is the work of unit A synced with what unit B does? how is the advancement? are the demands clear and understood? are there any obstacles? and more…, are all questions that must have specific and precise answers.


The research revealed a highly structured workplace. Even without digital tools, the Egyptians maintained a detailed communication through writing and signals.

![](https://archaeology.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Egypt-Wadi-el-Jarf-Accounts-Papyrus.jpg)

<br />

Scribes recorded the progress of work. For example, Merer's logbooks (4600 BC) note daily tasks: "Inspector Merer casts off with his phyle…loaded with stone, for Akhet Khufu; spends the night at the quarry; Day 27: sets sail toward Akhet Khufu, loaded with stone”.

Within the pyramids, graffiti inscriptions mark work gang names and dates. Recent reports note red-ink marking above the king's Chamber listing rotating gangs and days worked. Such notations effectively recorded which crews did which shifts.

Physical signals (like flags or bacon fires) are undocumented, but the Nile network itself was a communication 'highway’. Boats carried not just stone but also news and orders between quarries, harbors and Giza. Central administration in Memphis would dispatch instructions via couriers and boats to remote sites like Wadi el-Jarf or quarry camps.

<br />

Back then. In this equation, tools are nearly represented by the amount of people doing the management. In a way a task goes through several stages and each step is supervised by a person to ensure the quality and delivery. Instead of a shortcut system that combines the detailed constraints of the work which is the equivalent of management tools nowadays.
